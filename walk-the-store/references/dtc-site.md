# Website Walks (DTC, Shopify, SaaS)

The walk starts on Google, because that is where the persona starts. Then homepage, price, proof, product page, and the path to payment. Run the lean-back pass at phone width.

## The Google entry

- **Search the brand name.** Does the site own its own results? Do the meta title and description read like something a person wrote, or like a database export? Are sitelinks present? Are competitors buying ads on the brand name? For "[brand] reviews," what outranks the brand — review sites, Reddit threads, complaints?
- **Search the category phrase** in the persona's words. Does the brand appear at all, paid or organic? If the persona cannot find the product without already knowing its name, that finding usually leads the report's marketing section.

## The homepage, three-second read

- Above the fold: what is it, who is it for, why believe you. The persona reads the hero once — write down what they now think the product does, and verify later whether they were right.
- The primary CTA: what does it ask for, and what does it cost the persona? If "book a demo" is the only path forward, every sale routes through a calendar.
- Scroll with lean-back attention and mark the exact section where interest dies.

## Finding the price

- Count clicks from homepage to a number.
- **SaaS:** are tiers published at all? Published pricing filters unqualified demos and reads as confidence; a missing pricing page sends the persona to a competitor tab to calibrate, and they may not come back.
- **DTC:** is the shipping cost discoverable before checkout? Surprise shipping at the payment step is the classic abandonment trigger.
- Does the price shown here match the price shown on every other channel? Feed the answer into the consistency check.

## Proof

- **Demo content.** A product video near the hero; a live or interactive demo if the product supports one. The test: can a skeptical buyer try or watch the product at 11pm without talking to anyone?
- **Social proof.** Logos, testimonials with names and faces, case studies with numbers, review widgets with volume. Specific claims ("cut our close time from 9 days to 4") register as proof; generic praise registers as filler.
- **The FAQ reality check.** Does it answer the operational questions the persona actually has: integration steps, data handling, time to set up, what the buyer's team must do, returns and cancellation? An FAQ of softballs is a missed selling page.

## The product page

- Images: count, zoom, in-use shots, video; whether the persona can tell size, scale, and material without reading.
- Copy skim test: first line of each block only — does the skim sell?
- Specs and sizing complete enough that the persona never has to leave to verify something.
- Reviews on-page: volume, recency, and whether the worst review has a response.
- Shipping, returns, and stock state visible on the page, before the cart.

## The path to payment

Walk to the payment page and stop there. Count the steps, and note: forced account creation before checkout, guest checkout presence, express payments (Apple Pay, Shop Pay, PayPal), surprise costs appearing late, dead ends and error states. The lean-forward persona will restart a checkout once; a second failure ends the walk the way it ends real purchases.

## The mobile pass

Mandatory, and it belongs to lean-back mode: hero legibility at phone width, thumb-reach navigation, tap-target size, load feel on first paint, whether a sticky add-to-cart or CTA exists on long pages. Most first visits happen here; a desktop-only walk is half a walk.

## Trust markers

Contact findability (a real page, not a black hole), policy pages, security badges at checkout, footer completeness, copyright year (a stale year reads as an abandoned store), broken links, placeholder text. Small individually; the persona reads them in aggregate.
