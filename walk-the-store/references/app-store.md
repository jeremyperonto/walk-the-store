# App Walks (App Store, Google Play)

An app is merchandised in three places at once: Google results, the store listing, and the marketing site. Buyers cross all three, so the walk does too. The listing gets both passes — lean-back decides the tap, lean-forward decides the install.

## The Google entry

- **Search the app's name.** What wins the page — the store listing, the marketing site, or a competitor's ad on the brand term?
- **Search the problem** in the persona's words ("budget app that syncs accounts," "meal planner with grocery list"). Does the app surface anywhere: results, listicles, Reddit threads? For most apps this is where new users actually come from.

## The store search

- Search the category phrase inside the store itself. Where does the app rank, who sits above it, and are competitors running search ads on the app's own brand name?
- Icon test at grid size, next to the competitors that actually appear: distinct, legible, saying anything?

## The listing, lean-back

Three seconds, phone in hand:

- **Name/title** — around 30 characters, and the heaviest-weighted search field in the store. Brand only, or brand plus what it does? A bare brand name forfeits every search the persona runs.
- **Subtitle / short description** — indexed and skimmed. Does it say what the user gets, or is it brand copy that belongs on the website?
- **The first three screenshots.** Most shoppers never swipe past them. Do they tell a story in order, are captions legible at phone size, is the first one the product or a splash graphic? Preview video present?

## The listing, lean-forward

- **Ratings.** Count, average, recency. A 4.8 from 40 ratings loses to a 4.6 from 12,000; volume is the trust signal.
- **Reviews.** Read the top positive and the top negative in full — the persona will. Are there developer responses, and how old are they? Silence under bad reviews reads as abandonment.
- **What's New and the last-updated date.** More than about six months stale reads as risk on a paid app.
- **Description, first three lines.** Everything above the "more" fold is the entire description for most readers.
- **In-app purchase list.** Do the names and prices match the site's story, or do internal SKU names ("premium_tier_2") leak here?
- **Category choices.** Each category is browse surface. Is the primary right, and is there a legitimate second category going unused?

## The site cross-check

Same story, same pricing, same generation of screenshots on the marketing site and the listing? A site that promises features the screenshots don't show, or a price the IAP list contradicts, is a consistency finding — buyers hit both surfaces minutes apart.

## First run

If the app can be installed and walked: the listing sells the install, the first run sells the retention. Count screens between open and the first moment the product does its job. Note sign-up walls placed before any value, permission requests stacked at launch, and whether the first-run experience delivers what the screenshots promised.
